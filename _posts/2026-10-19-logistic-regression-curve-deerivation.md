I returned to learning some classical machine learning recently, mostly to brush up on some of the foundational math. I usually do this in order to not let my brain rot from just applying language models to any task that presents itself. One of the simpler initial concepts that people learn in classical ML is *Logistic Regression*. For the longest time, I had a very simplistic, incomplete, and frankly wrong, idea of what LR was actually doing, what it represented, and why it worked. Ironically, the most understanding I gained was from conversing with Claude, forcing it to slow down, and clarify every misunderstanding in excruciating detail until I was convinced that I understood. Of course, mere understanding is not enough, so I'd like to document it, in the hopes that future me (who will have forgotten this material all over again) will find this a valuable aid to recollection.
